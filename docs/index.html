THE IDEA

Better materials start
with a common language.

Artist-authored materials can look alike yet follow very different physical conventions. MIKU brings them into a shared representation before learning to generate.

Our unified PBR processing pipeline harmonizes geometry, texture, and material parameters across diverse sources. A two-stage framework then generates geometry-aware multi-view albedo and predicts metallic–roughness maps, encouraging consistent appearance across views and semantic object parts.

HOW IT WORKS

Unify. Generate. Refine.

Two complementary synthesis stages.

01 / THE FOUNDATION

A unified material space

Multi-source assets are rendered, filtered, and semantically annotated. Material normalization preserves high-frequency detail while aligning inconsistent artist-authored parameters.

  • 20 semantic material categories
  • Multi-view rendering and material segmentation
  • Iterative refinement and human validation
02 / APPEARANCE

Geometry-aware albedo

Reference appearance and explicit geometric guidance drive consistent multi-view synthesis. Packed views and geometry-conditioned guidance preserve object structure; distillation reduces sampling from 30 to 6 steps.

03 / MATERIAL

From appearance to intrinsic properties

A deterministic albedo-to-material model predicts metallic and roughness maps. Reference-image features combine semantic knowledge with appearance detail to respect material categories and part boundaries.

Scope & limitations

MIKU targets relative physical consistency, rather than recovery of physically measured material properties. Human validation adds cost and potential bias; measured materials and captured SVBRDFs remain directions for future work.
